# Market intel

Know the field before you pick a position.

## When to use

Two angles:
1. **Strategic** — build a competitive landscape, find white space, inform positioning.
2. **Tactical** — monitor specific competitors' pricing, content, ads, or feature changes.

Read `.agents/marketing-context.md` first to know the market and stage.

## Core frameworks

### Competitor map
List 5–10 competitors and classify them:
- **Direct** — same problem, same buyer
- **Indirect** — different solution, same problem
- **Substitutes** — what the customer does today (often "spreadsheets + manual work" or "do nothing")

For each: positioning statement (in their words), pricing model, target segment, distribution channels, strongest claim.

### Positioning map
Pick 2 axes that matter to buyers (e.g., price × ease-of-use, breadth × depth). Plot competitors. Look for empty quadrants — those are either gold or wasteland; figure out which.

### Pricing benchmark
Collect: list price, packaging structure, free tier or trial, discounts visible, hidden costs. Note the *anchor*: which price point appears most often.

### Differentiation audit
For each competitor, write one line: "Customers who pick them over us probably do it because ___." Then: "Customers who pick us over them probably do it because ___." If you can't fill either side, you have a positioning problem.

## Where to look

- Competitor websites, pricing pages, changelog, blog
- Review platforms (look for "vs" comparisons in reviews)
- LinkedIn (hiring posts reveal strategy)
- Search ads they're running (transparency centers on Google, Meta)
- Customer interviews — ask "who else did you look at, and why did you pick us?" 

## Compliance and limits

- Only public data.
- Comparative advertising must be truthful and not misleading (EU directive on misleading advertising; Polish: ustawa o zwalczaniu nieuczciwej konkurencji).
- Don't claim things you can't prove.

- Active regulations: Omnibus (lowest-price-from-30-days on every promotion), AI Act art. 50 (AI labeling from 2026-08-02), EAA/WCAG 2.2 (accessibility, since 2025-06-28), dyr. 2023/2673 (visible withdrawal button, no dark patterns, from 2026-06-19), GDPR/RODO baseline. Regulator: UOKiK and UODO.
- Polish consumers are research-driven and price-sensitive; social proof in Polish and trust signals (Sprawdzona Opinia, Ceneo reviews, BLIK + InPost as defaults) lift conversion materially.

## Questions to ask the user

1. Who are 3–5 competitors you care most about, and why those?
2. What decision do you need this analysis to support (positioning, pricing, feature roadmap, sales enablement)?
3. Any non-obvious substitutes (e.g., "doing it manually", "not solving it at all")?
