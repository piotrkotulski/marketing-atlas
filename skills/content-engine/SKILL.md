# Content engine

A system that produces content, not a list of posts.

## When to use

Read `.agents/marketing-context.md` first. If brand-positioning ran, use it. Match the user's language.

## Core frameworks

### 3–5 content pillars
Each pillar is a topic the brand owns. For each:
- **Why this pillar** — connects to a real customer job
- **Who reads it** — segment and stage of awareness
- **What we say that no one else does** — point of view
- **Cadence and format**

Example pillar split for a B2B SaaS founder: industry insights 30%, behind-the-scenes 25%, educational 25%, opinion 15%, product news 5%.

### Topic clusters (SEO-friendly)
- One **pillar page** per pillar (broad, long-form)
- 5–15 **cluster pages** linking back, covering specific subtopics
- Internal links flow toward the pillar
- Map each piece to search intent: informational, comparison, transactional

### Content atoms — one piece, many outputs
Every long-form piece (article, podcast, webinar) becomes:
- 2–4 short videos or audiograms
- 1 LinkedIn or Twitter thread
- 1 carousel
- 1 newsletter section
- 1 quote graphic

Plan the atoms when you plan the piece, not after.

### Editorial calendar that survives reality
- **Cadence** — weekly is realistic for most; daily only if there's a system
- **2-week buffer** — never publish from empty
- **Slots, not topics** — leave room for reactive posts
- **Owner per piece** — vague ownership = no publish

### Distribution > production
A good piece distributed across 5 channels beats 5 pieces published once. Plan distribution first.

## Compliance and limits

- AI Act art. 50: AI-generated content must be marked; keep an internal log of which pieces were AI-assisted.
- Cite sources; don't reuse copyrighted material verbatim.
- Environmental, health, financial claims: documented evidence.

## Questions to ask the user

1. What is the primary goal of content (traffic, leads, authority, community)?
2. Cadence and team size — what can you realistically publish each week?
3. Existing long-form material (podcasts, webinars, talks) we can repurpose?
4. Which platforms matter most — and which do not?
